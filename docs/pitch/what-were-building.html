The 60 second answer

If they lean in

In April 2026 one forged LayerZero message released about 116,500 rsETH, roughly $292M, from Kelp DAO's bridge, and holders on 20 chains lost value. The bridge had one verifier. Nothing checked whether the release was economically possible.

Chainlink just launched CCIP 2.0, where a token issuer can require its own verifier beside Chainlink's Committee Verifier. We built that verifier, and it enforces one law borrowed from Kirchhoff's circuit laws: what flows out of a junction must equal what flows in.

Our engine runs as Chainlink CRE workflows that read every chain with DON consensus. A forged credit is caught in the same workflow run it appears, the token is quarantined on every chain, and every CCIP transfer of it is refused. Lending markets read our feed and stop accepting it as collateral.

Two laws

Junction Rule, per message

Every credit on a destination chain must match exactly one finalized debit on the source chain: same message id, amount and recipient, never consumed before. A credit with no debit is forged. This is what catches the Kelp pattern on the very first transaction.

credit(id, amount) ⇒ ∃! debit(id, amount), finalized

Loop Rule, across every chain

Backing must cover everything that claims it. Escrow on the home chain must be at least the supply on every remote chain plus what is in flight. A deficit means value was created from nothing, on any bridge, even a compromised mint key with no message at all.

Δ = Escrow − (Σ Supply + In flight) ≥ 0

What happens when someone forges a message

  1. The forgery. A single verifier key signs a bridge credit with no matching burn. 116,500 kETH is released on Ethereum Sepolia.
  2. Detected in the same run. A CRE log trigger fires on the release, searches the source chains for the debit at finalized blocks, finds none, and writes a signed BREACH report to the ledger on all three chains.
  3. Contained everywhere. CCIP lanes freeze, the attacker is tainted, and the onchain Conservation Feed flips to BROKEN.
  4. The escape is refused. The attacker tries to bridge the stolen tokens through CCIP. Our verifier and token pool refuse, so the message never executes.
  5. Nobody lends against it. The token's own transfer guard blocks the tainted wallet, and a lending market reading our feed reverts every borrow.
  6. Recovery is human and slow by design. Only the issuer's 2 of 3 multisig can resolve, a timelock runs, and a CRE report alone can never clear BROKEN.

Why it is new

Nobody verifies economics

Bridges and verifier networks check who signed. Monitoring tools alert but cannot stop anything. We found no public CCV that checks economic conservation, and CCIP 2.0 opened that slot on September 28, 2026.

It sees every bridge

A bridge can only see its own messages. KIRCHHOFF watches total supply across all chains and all bridges, so an exploit on a different bridge is still stopped at the CCIP door.

AI onboards, math decides

A Spec Copilot turns a plain English description into a machine checked token spec with provenance on every line. AI never sits in the veto path; verdicts come only from deterministic math over consensus data.

Additive, never a replacement

We sit beside Chainlink's Committee Verifier. A transfer needs both signatures, so KIRCHHOFF only ever adds a second, independent reason to say no.

Under the hood

Chainlink CRE

Four TypeScript workflows: Junction Watch, Loop Ledger, Responder, Topology Watch. EVM log and cron triggers, consensus reads at pinned blocks, signed reports through the KeystoneForwarder.

CCIP 2.0 verifier

A policy hook running inside the official CCV Starter Kit cell, HMAC authenticated, reading status through two independent RPC providers, plus a CCIP token pool that enforces the same rule at execution time.

Onchain suite

Ledger, Quarantine, an AggregatorV3 compatible Conservation Feed, Registry with timelocked specs, transfer Guard. Solidity with unit, fuzz and invariant tests.

Mission Control

Tough questions, ready answers

Is this just monitoring?

No. Monitoring alerts, we veto. Our verifier withholds its signature inside CCIP, so a broken token's transfers do not execute. The hero moment of our demo is a refused transaction, not a chart.

What if your servers go down?

Nothing we operate alone can produce a verdict. Verdicts come from CRE DON consensus data and onchain state. If our UI or API is offline, every verdict still works, and if fresh data stops arriving the token fails closed by default.

Could your AI approve something bad?

AI has no write powers and no place in the veto path. It drafts specs and explains incidents, and every claim it makes must cite a tool result. We test it against prompt injection hidden in contract names and comments.

Can you undo the first fraudulent release?

Not on a bridge we do not sit on, and we say so openly. We contain it within one CRE run: the fake supply cannot spread through CCIP, cannot move on Guard enabled tokens, and cannot be borrowed against.

Why Chainlink?

CCIP 2.0 is the first place an issuer can require its own verifier on every message, and CRE gives us decentralized, consensus verified reads across many chains with signed onchain writes. The product does not exist without both.
